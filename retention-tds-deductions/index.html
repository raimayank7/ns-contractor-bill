Deductions on a contractor’s bill

The client rarely pays the full bill amount. Knowing each deduction lets you check the cheque and claim the money back later.

Retention or security deposit

A percentage (often 5% or 10%, as the contract says) is held back from each bill and paid after the defect liability period. It is still your income: track it, and ask for its release on time.

Income-tax TDS

A client paying a contractor deducts TDS: usually 1% when the contractor is an individual or HUF and 2% for others. The provision is now in section 393 of the Income-tax Act, 2025 (it was section 194C). When GST is shown separately on the bill, TDS is worked out on the value without GST, as CBDT explained in Circular 23/2017. The deduction shows in your Form 26AS / annual statement, and you claim it in your income tax return.

GST TDS

Government departments, local authorities and some public bodies deduct 2% GST TDS (1% CGST + 1% SGST, or 2% IGST) under section 51 of the CGST Act when the contract value is above ₹2.5 lakh. It is worked out on the taxable value. It reaches your electronic cash ledger once the client files its return; accept it on the GST portal.

Recoveries

  • Mobilisation advance: recovered in instalments from bills, as the contract sets.
  • Material issued by the client (cement, steel): its cost is recovered.
  • Other: water, electricity, penalties; type the reason so it prints.

Check the net

Net payable = bill amount (value + GST) − retention − TDS − GST TDS − recoveries. The maker prints each line, so you can compare it with the client’s payment advice.

Rates and thresholds come from the Income Tax and GST laws and the contract. Confirm with your accountant and your work order.
